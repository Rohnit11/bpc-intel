# Beauty & Personal Care in South Korea and India: A Comparative Market Study (2024–2026)

## TL;DR
- **South Korea is a mature (~US$13bn retail), slow-growing domestic market that has become the world's #2 cosmetics exporter (US$11.43bn in 2025); India is a fast-growing (~US$28–31bn), low-per-capita market with a decade-plus premiumization runway.** The two diverge most sharply on growth (Korea low-single-digit vs India low-double-digit) and structure (Korea export/innovation engine vs India domestic-demand story).
- **The confirmed strategic story in India is HUL's acquisition of Minimalist (Uprising Science) at a ~Rs 2,955 crore (~US$350m) pre-money valuation — NOT L'Oréal.** In Korea, the story is the ODM backbone (Cosmax, Kolmar) and record 2025 M&A — US$2.32bn of Korean beauty deals in Jan–Sep alone (McKinsey, via The Korea Herald).
- **Highest-conviction white space: derma/clinical skincare and sunscreen in India, ingestible/beauty-device adjacencies in Korea, and the K-beauty → India corridor via Nykaa/Tira.** Olive Young remains unlisted (IPO deferred) but is Korea's dominant channel and opens its first US store (Pasadena) in May 2026.

## 1. Scope, Taxonomy and Headline Market Sizing

### 1.1 Total market size — the anchor numbers
**South Korea.** Euromonitor International puts South Korea's total beauty and personal care (BPC) retail market at **~US$13 billion in 2024**, having grown just **1.8% in constant value terms** that year. This is the figure I weight most heavily for total-market size because it is Euromonitor's category-consistent BPC definition. Secondary market-research houses give a wide and inconsistent spread — Mordor Intelligence cites US$13.66bn (2025) growing at 6.61% to 2030; Expert Market Research cites US$18.39bn (2025) for "cosmetics." These higher figures use broader "cosmetics" definitions and should be treated cautiously; I weight Euromonitor's ~US$13bn BPC figure highest.

**India.** Estimates cluster in the **US$28–31bn range for 2024–2025** depending on definition. Statista's consumer-market data (cited by China Briefing) puts India BPC revenue at **US$33.08bn in 2025** (3.48% CAGR to 2030); the 2024 value was ~US$28bn. IMARC cites US$31.19bn (2025). I weight the ~US$28–31bn 2024–2025 range as the working figure. India's BPC market is therefore roughly **2.3–2.5x the size of Korea's** in absolute retail value, but that gap is misleading without the export and per-capita context below.

**The core divergence.** Korea's market grows low-single-digit (Euromonitor: 1.8% in 2024); India's grows low-double-digit (most houses cluster 10–11%; some conservative ones 5–6%). India's per-capita BPC spend is roughly US$22–23 (Statista: per-person revenue US$22.74 in 2025) versus Korea's ~US$250+ — an order-of-magnitude gap that is the single most important structural fact in this report: **India is a volume/penetration story; Korea is a value/innovation/export story.**

### 1.2 Sub-segment sizing and the retail/wholesale/export distinction
A critical caveat that runs through all beauty coverage: **retail value ≠ wholesale value ≠ export value.** Korea's US$11.43bn 2025 export figure (MFDS/Korea Customs Service, an FOB customs/wholesale value) is routinely conflated with domestic retail. They are different measures: the ~US$13bn is domestic retail sell-through; the US$11.43bn is cross-border shipment value; and Korea's domestic production value was KRW 17.9 trillion (2025, MFDS). I keep these separate throughout. On the India side, be alert that several "cosmetics" series (e.g., Grand View's ~US$21–27bn, Market Research Future's US$7.4bn "cosmetics") measure narrower baskets than the full BPC tree — do not compare across definitions.

## 2. South Korea Deep Dive

### 2.1 The domestic market: mature, premiumizing, dermocosmetics-led
Euromonitor characterizes 2024 as solid but decelerating: mass BPC outgrew premium (both grew), oral care and bath & shower slowed, post-pandemic colour-cosmetics momentum cooled, while **fragrances and sun care showed dynamic growth**. The standout is **dermocosmetics, which grew 13% in 2024** (Euromonitor) — driven by clinically-backed, results-oriented consumers and ingredient-led innovation around **PDRN, exosomes and peptides**, ingredients migrating from Korea's large aesthetic-surgery sector into topical skincare. LG Household & Health Care remained the overall #1 BPC company in Korea in 2024, followed by AmorePacific; they are the only two players with double-digit value share. AmorePacific holds ~22% of the premium segment (Euromonitor). "Skinimalism" (paring back routines to a few high-efficacy actives) and the aging population (fuelling anti-aging/"well-aging" and hair-loss functional lines) are the two demographic-facing consumer vectors; male beauty penetration in Korea is already the highest globally but is maturing rather than inflecting.

### 2.2 The K-beauty export engine
This is Korea's defining structural feature. Per MFDS/Korea Customs Service:
- **2025 exports: US$11.43 billion, a record, +12.3% YoY**, making Korea the **world's #2 cosmetics exporter**. Per MFDS data reported by UPI (May 28, 2026), Korea trailed "only runaway leader France with $24.3 billion," while "the United States ranked third with $10.8 billion, followed by Germany with $9.9 billion, Spain with $9.2 billion, Italy with $9 billion, China with $7.3 billion, and Japan with $3.9 billion."
- **Category split (2025, Yonhap via Korea Herald, Jan 9 2026):** "skin care products, such as moisturizers, rose 11.6 percent on-year to $8.54 billion. Color cosmetics shipments climbed 12 percent to $1.51 billion, while body cleansing products jumped 27.3 percent to $590 million. Fragrance exports surged 46.2 percent to $60 million." Skincare is thus ~75% of exports and colour ~13% — together ~88%.
- **Destination shift:** US became #1 at ~US$2.2bn (+15.1%); China fell to ~US$2.0bn (−19.2%). The combined US+China share fell to 36.7% from 43.1% in 2024 — the structurally important diversification story. Per Personal Care Magazine citing MFDS, "Korean cosmetics were shipped to 202 countries last year, up from 172 the year before," and September 2025 "posted the highest monthly figure at over $1.15 billion." Poland (+111.7% to US$282m) and UAE (+67.2% to US$286m) were breakout markets. Exports to India rose ~44.7% (H1 2025 basis) off a small base (~US$50m).
- **2024 exports were US$10.2bn** (+20.3%); domestic production hit a record KRW 17.54 trillion (2024) and KRW 17.9 trillion (2025).
- **Trade surplus crossed US$10.1 billion for the first time** in 2025. Per the Qatar Tribune citing MFDS, this was a "trade surplus of $10.1 billion in 2025, with imports totaling $1.29 billion... the first time the surplus exceeded $10 billion since South Korea first recorded a surplus of $90 million in 2012."

### 2.3 The ODM/OEM backbone — the "hidden" story
Korea's competitive moat is its contract-manufacturing ecosystem, which lets global indie brands, Amazon private labels and influencers launch K-quality product without owning factories:
- **Cosmax: record 2025 revenue ~KRW 2.39 trillion** (Korea Product Post; 2024 was KRW 2.16T, +21.9%, per Korea Biomedical Review). Cosmax holds ~14–18% of the Korean cosmetics ODM market (Future Market Insights).
- **Kolmar Korea: record 2025 revenue KRW 2.72 trillion, operating profit KRW 239.6bn** (Korea Biomedical Review). Kolmar became the first cosmetics ODM to enter Korea's "large conglomerate" ranks (group assets >KRW 5 trillion); its US subsidiary sales grew 71%.
- **Cosmecca Korea:** the smaller third player.
- On a production-value (not total revenue) basis, MFDS 2025 data ranked Cosmax #1 at KRW 1.61 trillion, Kolmar KRW 1.30 trillion, Cosmecca KRW 353 billion.

Both giants posted record Q1/Q2 2025 profits on indie-brand export demand; Kolmar and Cosmax shares rose ~88% and ~81% YTD by mid-2025 (KED Global). Kolmar was the exclusive manufacturing sponsor of "Amazon Beauty in Seoul" (Sept 2025). The strategic read: **the "marketing moat" is eroding and the "manufacturing moat" (formulas + smart factories) is the new source of durable advantage** — the "TSMC of beauty" framing. This has direct India relevance: Indian D2C brands and even Nykaa's owned labels can now source K-quality formulations via these ODMs.

### 2.4 Competitive landscape — the incumbents and the indie wave
**AmorePacific:** 2025 revenue KRW 4.25 trillion (+9.5%), operating profit KRW 335.8bn (+52.3%), net income KRW 247.3bn; group revenue KRW 4.6 trillion (its highest since before the pandemic). Overseas revenue +15%, with the Americas +20% and EMEA +42%; COSRX (acquired to 93.2% for ~US$560m/KRW 755bn in Oct 2024) and Laneige are the global growth engines. Its derma brands Aestura (Atobarrier365; launched at Sephora Jan 2025) and Illiyoon are scaling. Subsidiaries Innisfree (restructuring stores), Etude, espoir, Amos Professional and Osulloc round out the group.

**LG H&H:** the laggard and the restructuring story. 2025 full-year sales fell 6.7% to KRW 6.36 trillion; operating profit plunged 62.8% to KRW 170.7bn; Q4 swung to an operating loss. Weakness driven by China and a deliberate duty-free/offline restructuring (tightening distribution away from a volume-driven daigou past). Portfolio: The History of Whoo, O HUI, su:m37, belif, VDL, and hair-loss brand Dr. Groot (a US/Japan growth bright spot). Its Home Care & Daily Beauty (HDB) division houses mass personal care (oral, hair).

**The indie wave:** APR Corp (Medicube, at-home beauty devices) surpassed AmorePacific in market cap in 2025 (~KRW 8.6 trillion / US$5.86bn vs AmorePacific ~KRW 7 trillion / US$4.77bn per Personal Care Insights); its production value jumped 177.8% to KRW 285bn (rank 21→4). Other breakouts: The Founders (Anua, production +50% to KRW 228.5bn), Gudai Global (Beauty of Joseon, +68.6% to KRW 184.1bn), Benow (+52.9% to KRW 166.2bn) — most scaled via Olive Young and Amazon exports. Notably, d'Alba's production value roughly halved in 2025, illustrating the volatility of the "asset-light, marketing-heavy" indie model (Reach24H analysis of MFDS data).

### 2.5 Channels — Olive Young dominance, duty-free decline, e-commerce, live commerce
- **CJ Olive Young** is the dominant beauty channel: **FY2024 revenue KRW 4.79 trillion (+~22%); FY2025 KRW 5.83 trillion (+21.8%)** (Seoul Economic Daily), ~1,350–1,370 stores, and roughly **70%+ of Korea's brick-and-mortar health & beauty store channel** (a widely reported figure; the specific "71.3%" attribution circulating online traces to a non-authoritative wiki and should be verified against Passport). Foreign-tourist share of offline sales rose from 2% (2022) to 28% (2025), though >90% of revenue is still domestic. **IPO remains deferred** (preliminary review nearly filed then postponed in 2022; no confirmed 2026 date; a ~KRW 5 trillion valuation has been mooted). Per Yonhap (Jan 2026), "CJ Olive Young to open 1st U.S. outlet in May" — a Pasadena, California store anchoring a broader California rollout, supported by a Sephora curated-space arrangement. Olive Young's private-label brands (Bioheal BOH, Bringgreen, Wakemake, Round A'round) are themselves category forces.
- **Duty-free** has roughly halved from its 2019 peak (~KRW 24.8 trillion) to **~KRW 12.5 trillion in 2025 (−11.8%)** (Korea Duty Free Shops Association via Seoul Economic Daily), structurally impaired by the collapse of the Chinese daigou/代购 reseller model. Foreigners were ~74% of 2025 duty-free sales (KRW 9.33 trillion), and nearly half of Lotte Duty Free's 2024 sales came from Chinese resellers (WARC/Korea Herald). Korea's four major operators booked heavy losses in 2024 (Lotte alone KRW 143.1bn operating loss); Korea Customs reimposed purchase-quantity limits in Feb 2024. This is the single biggest downside channel risk for the prestige incumbents.
- **E-commerce** is now the largest BPC channel at **>50% of value (Euromonitor, 2024)**; Korea Ratings (via Korea JoongAng Daily) puts cosmetics online penetration at 60.8% in 2024 (up from 39.1% in 2019). Coupang (R.LUX luxury beauty), Naver (Plus Store, Brand Store), and increasingly Musinsa are the key platforms.
- **Live commerce** (~KRW 2.5 trillion in 2024; sizing varies by scope definition) is dominated by Naver Shopping Live (~73.6% primary-destination share), with Kakao Shopping Live #2 and beauty a top category.
- **Road shops** (single-brand stores) have collapsed: Innisfree sales fell from a KRW 768bn peak (2016) to the ~KRW 200bn range (2022–2024; FY24 KRW 224.6bn, −18%); Missha halved from ~800 to ~400 stores. THAAD (2017 loss of Chinese tourists), COVID, and the rise of Olive Young + online drove the shift.

### 2.6 Regulation (MFDS)
Korea regulates "functional cosmetics" (whitening, wrinkle improvement, UV protection, hair-loss relief, hair dye) via the MFDS Korean Functional Cosmetics Codex (KFCC); ingredients not on the KFCC require MFDS evaluation. In H1 2024, MFDS/NIFDS evaluated 572 functional-cosmetic cases (UV filters largest, then hair dyes). A **domestic cosmetics safety-assessment system** will pilot 2026–2027, phase in from 2028 (first covering companies with annual production/import >KRW 1bn, plus newly approved functional and infant/child products), and reach full enforcement by 2031 (Trade.gov/MFDS). Korea banned animal-tested cosmetics and pursues regulatory-diplomacy MOUs (e.g., Philippines FDA) to smooth exports.

## 3. India Deep Dive

### 3.1 Market and premiumization runway
India's ~US$28–31bn BPC market is defined by low penetration and a long premiumization runway. Skincare/sun care leads (~35% by IMARC's BPC segmentation; skincare ~49% of the narrower "cosmetics" basket per Grand View). The mass tier still dominates colour cosmetics (~79.55% mass in 2025, Mordor). Digital channels are projected at ~9.4% of revenue in 2025. Growth is concentrating in **masstige and premium** tiers and in **Tier 2/3 cities**, where q-commerce and vernacular marketing are opening trusted-brand access for the first time.

### 3.2 Competitive landscape and the D2C consolidation wave
- **Hindustan Unilever (HUL):** the incumbent (Dove, Pond's, Lakmé), pressured by D2C challengers. **Confirmed: HUL acquired 90.5% of Uprising Science (Minimalist).** Per Business Standard (Apr 22, 2025), HUL "announced the completion of its acquisition of a 90.5% stake in Uprising Science, the parent company of the skincare brand Minimalist, for Rs 2,706.44 crore... executed through a combination of primary infusion and secondary share purchase." The signed deal (Jan 2025) valued Minimalist at a Rs 2,955 crore (~US$350m) pre-money EV, with the remaining 9.5% to be acquired "in about two years" (Entrackr); Minimalist revenue "surged to Rs 347 crore in FY24 from Rs 184 crore in FY23." **This corrects the brief's premise — it was HUL, not L'Oréal, that bought Minimalist.** L'Oréal was not the acquirer of Minimalist/Uprising Science.
- **Nykaa (FSN E-Commerce):** FY25 revenue ~Rs 10,022 crore; Q4 FY25 revenue Rs 2,062 crore (+24%), net profit Rs 20 crore (+193%); Q2 FY26 net profit Rs 34.4 crore (3.4x YoY). 265 stores across 90 cities, ~4,200 beauty brands, ~40m beauty customers. Beauty is the profit engine; House of Nykaa owned brands (5x YoY salience growth in H1 FY26) and B2B arm Superstore (276k retailers across 1,100 cities) are scaling. It is both retailer and house-of-brands.
- **Honasa Consumer (Mamaearth):** FY25 revenue Rs 2,067 crore (+8%), PAT Rs 73 crore (−32%, reflecting a distribution-model reset, "Project Neev"). Portfolio: Mamaearth, The Derma Co., Aqualogica, Dr. Sheth's, BBlunt. Recovering strongly in FY26 (Q4 FY26 revenue Rs 657 crore, +23%; PAT +178%).
- **Others:** Lakmé/Tata; L'Oréal India (opened an R&D centre in 2024 and a Beauty Tech hub; entered dermocosmetics via L'Oréal Dermatological Beauty in 2023); Plum (Unilever reportedly acquired a majority stake, ~US$180m, in 2025 — verify terms); SUGAR (L Catterton-backed, Series D US$50m); Purplle (unicorn, ~US$1.1bn valuation, ADIA-led US$120m round in 2024); and the retail contest Nykaa vs Amazon vs Flipkart vs Tira (Reliance).
- **D2C funding** cooled sharply into 2026 (Tracxn: D2C beauty funding down ~83% YoY in early 2026), signaling a shift from a funding phase to a consolidation phase — with strategics (HUL, Unilever, L'Oréal) and platforms (Nykaa's Dot & Key stake) as the consolidators.

### 3.3 The mass–masstige–premium ladder and channels
Growth is concentrating up-ladder: mass still carries volume (general trade), but value growth and new-brand formation is in masstige (Mamaearth, Plum, SUGAR) and premium/derma (Minimalist, The Derma Co., Illiyoon, Kama). Channels:
- **Quick commerce** is the fastest-rising beauty channel: BPC is Blinkit's second-largest category at 13.4% of daily sales (Unicommerce); the Indian q-commerce market is ~US$5.5bn (2025) with Blinkit >50% share, Zepto and Instamart contesting #2. Nykaa launched "Nykaa Now" 10-minute delivery (Mumbai pilot, ~5% of SKUs). Fastest-growing beauty sub-categories on q-commerce per Nykaa Superstore data include deo roll-ons (>100% YoY) and sunscreens.
- **Nykaa vs Amazon vs Flipkart** dominate online; **general trade** still carries the bulk of mass volume; **beauty advisors/assisted selling** remain important in premium and at Nykaa/Tira/Sephora physical stores.

### 3.4 Consumer trends and regulation
- **Trends:** ayurvedic/natural positioning (Patanjali, Dabur, Forest Essentials, Kama Ayurveda — L'Oréal reportedly took ~30% of Kama for ~US$100m); K-beauty penetration (see §4.1); ingredient-conscious Gen Z; men's grooming inflection (Euromonitor: men's grooming Rs 186.3bn in 2025, +9%; IMARC's narrower "male grooming products" basket US$2.3bn in 2024, 6.8% CAGR); and regional/vernacular marketing into Tier 2/3.
- **Regulation:** CDSCO under the Drugs & Cosmetics Act 1940 and Cosmetics Rules 2020; imported cosmetics require registration (Form COS-1 application → COS-2 Registration Certificate) via the online SUGAM portal, plus registration of the manufacturing site; BIS sets IS 4707 ingredient standards (revised Aug 2025 for IS 4707 Part 2). Paper filings are no longer accepted as of 2026. This mandatory import-registration regime — pre-clearance before any consignment — is the key friction and lead-time cost for K-beauty brands entering India.

## 4. Cross-Cutting Modules

### 4.1 Korea → India corridor
Nykaa is the primary conduit, via a decade-long (since 2016) partnership with AmorePacific: it has launched Innisfree (2016), then Laneige, Sulwhasoo, COSRX, Aestura, Illiyoon, and in 2025 Korean haircare (Mise En Scène, RYO) — now hosting eight AmorePacific brands. Other K-brands (Anua, Some By Mi, Etude, Isntree, Dr.Jart+, The Face Shop) enter via Nykaa, Amazon, and Reliance's Tira (which launched Mixsoon in Aug 2024). **Whitespace:** mid-price derma K-beauty, dedicated sunscreens, and men's K-beauty remain under-penetrated in India's Tier 2/3, where q-commerce now provides the access rail. The CDSCO registration lead-time is the main entry barrier, favouring brands that partner with an established Indian importer/platform.

### 4.2 Value chain map (named players)
- **Korea:** Ingredients/actives (PDRN, exosome, peptide suppliers) → ODM/OEM (Cosmax, Kolmar Korea, Cosmecca) → brands (AmorePacific, LG H&H, APR, Gudai Global, The Founders) → distribution (Silicon2 and other export distributors) → retail (Olive Young, Coupang, Naver, duty-free, and abroad Amazon/Sephora/Olive Young US).
- **India:** Ingredients/contract manufacturers (often importing formulations or sourcing via Korean ODMs) → brands (HUL, L'Oréal India, Nykaa house brands, Honasa, SUGAR, Plum) → distribution (general trade distributors, q-commerce dark stores) → retail (Nykaa, Tira, Amazon, Flipkart, Blinkit/Zepto/Instamart, general trade).

### 4.3 M&A and investment (last 3 years)
- **Korea:** K-beauty M&A hit a post-pandemic record in 2025. Per McKinsey via The Korea Herald (Nov 3, 2025), "beauty-sector mergers and acquisitions in Korea totaled $2.32 billion between January and September, surpassing the food and beverage sector's $977 million"; ION Analytics/Mergermarket separately counted "26 deals worth ~US$1.8bn." Landmark deals: AmorePacific/COSRX (~US$560m to 93.2%, Oct 2024); Gudai Global buying Skinfood + Serin (~KRW 750bn / US$521m); KKR/Samhwa (cosmetics packaging, KRW 733bn, Sept 2025); Blackstone/Juno Hair (KRW 800bn, Sept 2025); L'Oréal/Dr.G (Gowoonsesang, Dec 2024). Valuation multiples are mostly undisclosed for the Korean deals.
- **India:** HUL/Minimalist (Rs 2,706.44 crore for 90.5%; ~Rs 2,955 crore / US$350m pre-money EV); Unilever/Plum (~US$180m majority, reported — verify); L'Oréal/Kama Ayurveda (~30%, ~US$100m, reported); SUGAR (L Catterton Series D US$50m); Purplle (ADIA-led US$120m, 2024). As a global reference multiple, L'Oréal's US$1.1bn Medik8 deal was pegged at ~13x 2024 sales — indicative of the premium being paid for science-backed skincare that Korean and Indian derma brands are riding.

### 4.4 Five-year outlook (base/bull/bear)
- **Korea — base:** low-single-digit domestic growth; exports compounding high-single/low-double digit toward ~US$15bn+ by 2030 as US/Europe/Middle East offset China. **Bull:** diversification accelerates, ODM capacity scales, exports >US$18bn. **Bear:** US tariff exposure plus continued China structural decline drag exports flat and squeeze duty-free-dependent incumbents. Swing factors: (1) US demand and tariff policy, (2) China trajectory, (3) ODM capacity/margins.
- **India — base:** ~10% BPC growth, premiumization + q-commerce compounding. **Bull:** premium/derma inflection plus Tier 2/3 digital access push growth to low-teens. **Bear:** mass-consumption slowdown, D2C funding winter, and channel-economics margin pressure hold growth to mid-single digits. Swing factors: (1) rural/mass demand recovery, (2) premiumization pace, (3) q-commerce/channel profitability.

### 4.5 White-space synthesis (5 most attractive, ranked)
1. **India × dermocosmetics/clinical skincare** — fastest-growing, under-supplied, and now strategically validated (Minimalist, The Derma Co., Illiyoon). Highest conviction.
2. **India × sunscreen/sun care** — low penetration, rising UV/pollution awareness, meaningful ASP uplift; already among the fastest q-commerce risers.
3. **Korea × beauty devices + ingestibles/nutricosmetics** — APR's device success (KRW 285bn production, +178%) shows the device category has "trillion-won" headroom; ingestibles are the adjacent play.
4. **India × men's grooming (skincare/cosmetics, not just toiletries)** — 9%+ growth off a low base; men's skincare specifically is under-served vs shave/toiletries.
5. **Korea→India K-beauty derma via q-commerce/Tira** — masstige-price derma K-beauty delivered through dark stores to Tier 2/3 is the corridor's clearest whitespace.
